1. THE IDENTITY — is the staking RATE correlated with the staking YIELD?
==============================================================================
   Yes, and not statistically: by construction. Ethereum pays
   I(S) = 166.28*sqrt(S) ETH/yr on a staked balance S, so
   APR(S) = I(S)/S = 166.28/sqrt(S), i.e. log APR = log 166.28 - 0.5*log S.
   The regression below is a unit test of the reconstruction, not a finding:
   a slope of exactly -0.5 and R² = 1 is what SHOULD come out.

  log(consensus APR)  ~  log(ETH staked)
    n = 43   R² = 1.0000   r = -1.000   Durbin-Watson = 1.70
    slope   -0.5000  per log(ETH)   SE 0.0000   t = -77578.40   p = 0.000***
    95% CI  [-0.5000, -0.5000]
    HAC(L=3)  SE 0.0000   t = -81443.32   p = 0.000
    theory: slope = -0.5000, intercept = log(166.28) = 5.1140
    fitted: slope = -0.5000, intercept = 5.1139   -> deviation 3.72e-06

   Elasticity reading: a 1% rise in the staked balance lowers the consensus
   APR by 0.5%. Doubling the stake cuts the yield by 29.3%. Quadrupling it
   halves the yield. That is the whole of the stake-rate/yield relationship —
   there is no behavioural content in it, and nothing to estimate.

2. LEVELS — the naive regression, shown so it can be ruled out
==============================================================================
  ETH price ($)  ~  consensus APR (%)
    n = 43   R² = 0.1683   r = -0.410   Durbin-Watson = 0.40
    slope   -871.3630 $ per 1pp APR   SE 302.5055   t = -2.88   p = 0.006***
    95% CI  [-1482.2852, -260.4407]
    HAC(L=3)  SE 366.5470   t = -2.38   p = 0.022

  ETH/BTC (x1000)  ~  consensus APR (%)
    n = 43   R² = 0.6972   r = +0.835   Durbin-Watson = 0.27
    slope   +33.4501 mBTC  per 1pp APR   SE 3.4426   t = +9.72   p = 0.000***
    95% CI  [+26.4977, +40.4026]
    HAC(L=3)  SE 4.0347   t = +8.29   p = 0.000

   Durbin-Watson 0.40 and 0.27. Both are far below 1.
   Residuals are strongly autocorrelated, which is the signature of a
   spurious regression: two trending series, no relationship. The R² of
   0.70 on ETH/BTC is a coincidence of timing, not evidence. It
   would license the claim 'falling yield caused ETH/BTC to fall'. Do not
   publish either of these.

==============================================================================
  ETH monthly return (%)  ~  ΔAPR (bps)
    n = 42   R² = 0.0030   r = -0.055   Durbin-Watson = 1.75
    slope   -0.1802 pp  per bp   SE 0.5212   t = -0.35   p = 0.731
    95% CI  [-1.2335, +0.8731]
    HAC(L=3)  SE 0.3435   t = -0.52   p = 0.603

  ETH/BTC monthly return (%)  ~  ΔAPR (bps)
    n = 42   R² = 0.0021   r = -0.046   Durbin-Watson = 1.65
    slope   -0.0968 pp  per bp   SE 0.3296   t = -0.29   p = 0.771
    95% CI  [-0.7630, +0.5695]
    HAC(L=3)  SE 0.1494   t = -0.65   p = 0.521

   Neither slope is distinguishable from zero at any conventional level,
   under OLS or under HAC standard errors. The R² are effectively nil:
   changes in staking yield explain 0.30% of the variance in
   ETH's monthly return and 0.21% of ETH/BTC's.

5. POWER — could this test have found an effect if one existed?
==============================================================================
  residual σ of ETH monthly return   18.10 pp
  σ of ΔAPR                          5.42 bps
  smallest slope detectable at 5% / 80% power   1.4603 pp per bp

  Over the sample, APR fell 148 bps in total. Applying the
  slope linearly across that decline: a slope at the detection threshold
  would have moved ETH by 216 pp cumulatively; the point estimate implies
  27 pp, and the 95% CI spans -129 to +183 pp — i.e. it contains
  everything from a large positive to a large negative effect.

  Honest reading: monthly data cannot rule out a small yield effect. What it
  rules out is a LARGE one. A 148 bp decline in yield delivered by the
  existing curve produced no detectable price response, so the prior that a
  further ~130 bp cut from EIP-8363 will move price has no support here —
  and the burden of proof sits with whoever claims otherwise.
